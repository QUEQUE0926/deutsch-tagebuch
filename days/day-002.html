Grammar Tap ① · "Ich habe eine Frage." — The indefinite article ein/eine/ein

Layer 1 · Sentence breakdown

Ich | habe | eine | Frage. I | have | a | question. [Subject] [Verb slot 2] [Indef. article] [Noun]

German has THREE indefinite articles because nouns have three genders. They all mean "a/an", but each matches its noun's gender:

ein + masculine → ein Pass, ein Koffer, ein Bahnhof eine + feminine → eine Frage, eine Antwort, eine Familie ein + neuter → ein Ticket, ein Taxi, ein Problem

Layer 2 · Word annotation

  • ich — 1st sg., "I"
  • habe — 1st sg. of haben, "have" (the irregular verb; Day 3 covers it formally)
  • eine — indefinite article, feminine, nominative
  • Frage — feminine noun, "question"

Layer 3 · Study tip · Why gender matters from Day 1

English lets you say "a question / a ticket / a passport" with the same "a". German forces you to pick the matching article. If you learn "Frage" without "die", you can't say "a question" correctly later. Always learn the noun WITH its definite article — the indefinite article follows from it automatically.

der → ein  ·  die → eine  ·  das → ein   (just drop the "d" and add "-e" for feminine)
Grammar Tap ② · "Ich suche den Bahnhof." — Present tense verb conjugation

Layer 1 · Sentence breakdown

Ich | suche | den | Bahnhof. I | look-for | the | train station. [Subject] [Verb slot 2] [Def. article] [Noun]

Most German verbs follow a regular pattern in the present tense. Take the stem (suchen → such-) and add endings:

ich such-e wir such-en du such-st ihr such-t er/sie such-t sie/Sie such-en

Layer 2 · Word annotation

  • suche — 1st sg. of suchen (+ Akk.), "look for"
  • den — masculine definite article, Akkusativ (you'll learn cases in Day 31+; for now notice der → den when it's the object)
  • Bahnhof — masculine noun, "train station"

Layer 3 · Study tip · Only one verb form is irregular per verb

For regular verbs, the only form that changes the stem is du: it adds -st. Everything else is just the stem + a vowel ending (-e, -en, -t). So once you know "ich suche", you basically know the whole verb.

Same pattern for today's verbs: finden → ich finde, du findest · nehmen → ich nehme, du nimmst (⚠️ nehmen is irregular: du nimmst, er nimmt — vowel changes e→i)

3. Listening · 机场广播

Listen to an airport announcement at BER. Pick your speed, then answer the questions. The transcript stays hidden until you finish — listen carefully first.

🔊 Airport announcement (≈55 words)

Speed: 0.5× 0.75× 1× 1.25×

Listen first, answer the quiz, then you may open the transcript to check.

Q1 · Number — Which platform (Gleis) does the train depart from?

  • A) Three
  • B) Four
  • C) Five
Show answer & explanation
Answer: B (Four)
The announcement says "Der Zug … fährt von Gleis vier." → Gleis 4 = platform 4. German numbers: drei=3, vier=4, fünf=5.
If wrong: ☐ 单词不认识 (didn't know Gleis) ☐ 语法没懂 ☐ 理解偏了 ☐ 没听清 → re-listen at 0.5× to the phrase "Gleis vier"

Q2 · True or False — The taxi is cheaper than the train.

Show answer & explanation
Answer: False
The announcement says "Die Bahn ist schneller und billiger" (the train is faster and cheaper) — so the taxi is the more expensive option. It costs "etwa fünfundvierzig Euro" (about €45).
If wrong: ☐ 单词不认识 (didn't know billiger) ☐ 语法没懂 ☐ 理解偏了 ☐ 没听清 → re-listen at 0.5× to "billiger"

Q3 · Number — How long is the train ride?

  • A) 13 minutes
  • B) 30 minutes
  • C) 40 minutes
Show answer & explanation
Answer: B (30 minutes)
"Die Fahrtzeit beträgt dreißig Minuten." → dreißig = 30. Watch out: dreizehn = 13, dreißig = 30 — German numbers can sound alike at normal speed.
If wrong: ☐ 单词不认识 (didn't know dreißig) ☐ 语法没懂 ☐ 理解偏了 (confused 13/30) ☐ 没听清 → re-listen at 0.5× to "dreißig Minuten"
Show transcript (after answering)

🔊Achtung! Der Zug nach Berlin Hauptbahnhof fährt von Gleis vier. Die Fahrtzeit beträgt dreißig Minuten. Bitte beachten Sie: Das Taxi vor dem Terminal kostet etwa fünfundvierzig Euro. Die Bahn ist schneller und billiger. Vielen Dank.

English: Attention! The train to Berlin Central Station departs from platform 4. The travel time is 30 minutes. Please note: the taxi in front of the terminal costs about 45 euros. The train is faster and cheaper. Thank you.

Day 2 · Pronunciation self-check · ch 两音（ich-Laut / ach-Laut）

German "ch" has TWO sounds — which one you use depends on the vowel before it. Today's words suchen, mich, ich use the front (soft) one; noch, auch use the back (hard) one.

SoundWhenExampleHow to produce
ich-Laut [ç]after front vowels (i, e, ü, ö), after l/r/n, and in suffix -chen🔊 ich, 🔊 mich, 🔊 su-chenSay English "huge" and hold the "h" — that hissy friction is [ç]. Or say "cat" and slide the "y" forward.
ach-Laut [x]after back vowels (a, o, u, au)🔊 noch, 🔊 auch, 🔊 BuchLike the "ch" in Scottish "loch" — a soft scraping at the back of the throat. NOT a hard English "k".

Tip: If you accidentally use the wrong one, Germans still understand you — but getting it right makes you sound much more natural. Practice the minimal pair: 🔊 ich vs 🔊 ach.
