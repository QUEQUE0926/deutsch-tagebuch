📖 Full translation · English (translate it yourself first, then check)

¶1 — This evening I was at a restaurant in Berlin with Anna. The restaurant is called "Bei Luigi" and it is very small, but cozy. The waiter was very friendly.

¶2 — I was hungry and ordered a pizza with tomatoes. Anna took the soup and a salad. I drank a coffee with it. The coffee was strong, but very good. I really enjoyed the pizza — it was hot and delicious!

¶3 — Anna liked the soup, but the salad was cold. She said: "The salad is not so good." I gave her some of my pizza. We laughed.

¶4 — In the end we got the bill. The meal cost 28 euros altogether. I paid and gave the waiter three euros as a tip. It was a lovely evening in Berlin.

🧩 Grammar in context · 3 points from today's text

"Heute Abend war ich mit Anna in einem Restaurant in Berlin."

1 · Präteritum of sein and haben — In diaries and stories, war (was) and hatte (had) are the normal past forms of sein and haben: ich war, ich hatte. German almost never says "bin gewesen" or "habe gehabt" — one short word does the whole job, and you will meet these two forms in nearly every past-tense sentence you ever read.

"Das Essen kostete zusammen 28 Euro."

2 · Simple past: regular -te vs. strong verbs — Most verbs build the past with -te: bestellen → bestellte, bezahlen → bezahlte, schmecken → schmeckte. A few very common verbs are strong — they change their vowel instead: nehmen → nahm, geben → gab, trinken → trank. Both types are Präteritum, the tense this diary is written in.

"Ich bezahlte und gab dem Kellner drei Euro Trinkgeld."

3 · Dativ person + Akkusativ thing — geben takes two objects: the receiver (a person) is Dativ — dem Kellner; the thing given is Akkusativ — drei Euro Trinkgeld (人三物四). The same pattern appears earlier: ich gab ihr etwas — ihr is the Dativ of sie. Bonus: schmecken works the same way — die Pizza schmeckte mir sehr (mir = to me, Dativ).

Self-check rubric

  • ☐ Register: Sie form throughout (Ihre, Sie, sehr geehrte)

  • ☐ At least one modal-like polite form: möchte / würden Sie
  • ☐ Articles present on all nouns (der Tisch, die Reservierung, …)

I would like to reserve a table for three people on Saturday, 5 October, at 19:00. We would prefer to sit by the window, if possible. One companion is vegetarian — is there a vegetarian option on the menu?

Vielen Dank im Voraus. Ich freue mich auf Ihre Bestätigung.

Many thanks in advance. I look forward to your confirmation.

Mit freundlichen Grüßen
Tom Becker

Yours sincerely,

亮点句式:

  • «ich möchte gerne ... reservieren» — 礼貌请求（möchte + gerne + 动词不定式）
  • «Wir würden gerne ... sitzen» — 第二虚拟式 würden + gerne，比 möchten 更正式
  • «gibt es ...?» — 倒装问句，询问有无
